title: Back to My Roots
layout: post
---
The tree swayed unsteadily, fruit against leaf against branch.








From the highest point of the tree, it could see everything.

The unruly long grass, the golden fields beyond, the clear blue skies. The little house beneath the hill. The hustle of people passing underneath.

It has always been afraid of leaving its home, the sturdy trunk holding them afloat, but it is even more afraid of heights.

Which is ironic, as the height of its home is the only thing keeping it with its beloved tree.

Apple and tree, tree and apple.

One and the same.

One and the same?

It was the reason why existed, and the reason why I kept existing.

So it stayed.

Clinging onto the branch, listening to the whistling of the wind, studying the motions of the people underneath.

Watching as the other apples slowly disappeared, one by one.

It pitied them. Why would you want to leave the branch that had grown them?

Watching, waiting, as the warmth of the summer faded, like the dying light in someone’s eyes.

Like the dying light on its plump, red skin.

Something was changing.

The strong flesh inside the apple’s healthy cover was corrupting, the life draining from it.

Perhaps it only looks healthy on the outside. The sunlight still touched its skin, it still watched the people passed underneath, listened to the whistling of the soft breeze.

It was withering away, succumbing, and even though the flesh inside was browning and hollow, it felt.

For the first time.

It felt heavy.

Like the lack of weightlessness suddenly descended upon it, crashing into it, drowning it, devouring it.

And it started falling.

Falling through the air, tumbling through those leaves, brushing against that familiar yet not so familiar trunk.

It felt heavy, but light.

It was such a weird feeling.

There was a soft thud.

Then silence.

It would never have to be afraid of heights again.

As seasons spiralled through, losing track of time, the young tree swayed unsteadily, fruit against leaf against branch.

New apples bloomed like orchards.

The apple stared down at the unruly long grass, the golden fields beyond, the clear blue skies. The little house beneath the hill. The hustle of people passing underneath.

The leaves of the young tree tangled with those of the old, like a bittersweet embrace.

Apple and tree, one and the same.

It never wanted to leave. Who would? When you have warmth and life and family.

But the apple never falls far from the tree anyways.
